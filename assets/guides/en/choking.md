# CHOKING

Choking
What to do when someone cannot cough, speak, or breathe.

---

## Check if it is serious

**If the person can cough forcefully or speak, do not hit their back or squeeze them. Encourage them to keep coughing.**

Signs of severe choking: they cannot speak, cough, or breathe; they make no sound or a high-pitched noise; they grab their throat; their lips or skin turn blue.

## If they cannot cough, speak, or breathe

1. Shout for help and ask someone to call emergency services.
2. Lean the person forward. Give **5 firm back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. Then give **5 abdominal thrusts**: stand behind them, place a fist just above their belly button and below the rib cage, grasp it with your other hand, and pull sharply inward and upward.
4. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.

## If they are pregnant or very large

- Give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts. Place your hands on the center of the chest and pull straight back.

## If they become unresponsive

1. Lower them gently to the ground and make sure emergency services have been called.
2. Start chest compressions as in CPR.
3. Each time you open the airway, look in the mouth. Remove an object only if you can see it. Do not sweep blindly with your finger.

## Good to know

- For a choking infant (under 1 year), the technique is different. Tell the call-taker immediately so they can coach you.
- Even if the object comes out, the person should be checked by a medical professional, especially after abdominal thrusts.
