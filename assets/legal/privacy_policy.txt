# 1. Introduction

The Saup Angeles ("MEAS," "the System," "the Application," "we," "us," or "our") respects your privacy and is committed to protecting your personal information.

Saup Angeles is a localized capstone and pilot system designed to assist users in preparing and submitting structured emergency information to participating emergency institutions in Angeles City, Philippines.

This Privacy Policy explains how Saup Angeles collects, uses, stores, protects, and may disclose personal information when you create an account, verify your identity, use the Application, submit an emergency report, use Pre-Call Guidance, use location-based features, or otherwise interact with the System. It applies to public users, to users under 18 and their guardians, and to responders who use the Application.

This Privacy Policy should be read together with the **Saup Angeles Terms and Conditions**.

Saup Angeles is intended to follow the principles and requirements of **Republic Act No. 10173, otherwise known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (DPA)**, its Implementing Rules and Regulations, and applicable issuances of the National Privacy Commission (NPC).

The DPA recognizes the rights of individuals over their personal information and requires processing to observe the principles of transparency, legitimate purpose, and proportionality.

This Privacy Policy is currently available in English only.

# 3. What Personal Information Does Saup Angeles Collect?

The information collected depends on the features that you use.

## 3.1 Account and Identification Information

When you create and set up an account, Saup Angeles collects:

* First name, middle name, and last name;
* Email address, and your sign-in method (email and password, or Google Sign-In);
* Contact number (verified using a one-time code sent by SMS);
* Date of birth and sex;
* Home address (house number, street, and barangay);
* Nationality, civil status, and religion;
* Identification type;
* Your identification number, which is **not stored in full**. Only a keyed one-way hash (used to detect duplicate accounts) and the last four characters are kept;
* A photograph of your identification document, where required;
* A selfie, which is also used as your profile photograph;
* Your verification status, and any decision, reason, or note recorded by the reviewer; and
* Account identifiers and your language preference (English or Filipino).

If you sign in with Google, Saup Angeles receives the name and email address associated with your Google account.

---

## 3.2 Health-Related and Emergency Information

You may choose to provide the following, all of which are optional:

* Blood type;
* Height and weight;
* Vaccination status, vaccine type, and vaccination date; and
* Your choice on whether the medical responder assigned to your emergency may view your blood type, height, and weight (see "Medical Information Sharing with Responders").

When you submit an emergency report, you may also provide information about victims and the condition of the people involved.

Some information in this category, including health information, is **sensitive personal information** under the Data Privacy Act. Other information the System collects may also be sensitive personal information, such as date of birth or age, civil status, religion, and government-issued identification details.

Users should provide only information that is necessary and relevant to the intended purpose.

---

## 3.3 Emergency Report Information

When you submit an emergency report, Saup Angeles collects:

* Report reference number and report type;
* Incident type (medical, police, or fire) and any police concern type;
* Incident description and additional information;
* Answers to Pre-Call Guidance questions (yes/no answers);
* Victim information (name and description);
* Whether you are reporting for yourself or for someone else;
* Reported location, the original GPS location, the location accuracy, and any landmark or location note you add;
* Photographs you attach;
* The priority assigned to the report, any additional services involved, and the station the report is routed to;
* Date and time information and report status; and
* Information associated with the submitting user's account.

For each service involved in a report (for example, medical and police), Saup Angeles also keeps a response record containing the assigned station and responder, status changes with timestamps, and any notes or photographs added by the responder.

Reports logged by a dispatcher from an SMS may not be linked to an app account.

---

## 3.4 Location Information

Saup Angeles collects location information when you use location-based features.

This includes:

* Your device's current location, taken as a one-time reading when you open the relevant screen or submit a report;
* The location attached to an emergency report, and any manual adjustment you make to it;
* Location information displayed on maps; and
* Information used to identify nearby participating emergency services.

For ordinary users, Saup Angeles does not continuously track your location in the background.

If you are a responder, your location is shared continuously during an active case (see "Responders and Live Location Sharing").

Location information is collected only when you grant the necessary device permissions. You may also be able to manually select or adjust an incident location.

Because GPS and other location technologies are not always precise, the location stored by Saup Angeles may not exactly represent the actual location of the user or incident.

---

## 3.5 Photographs and Other Attachments

Saup Angeles allows users to attach photographs to emergency reports. Responders assigned to a case may also add photographs and notes to it.

Attachments may contain personal information or information concerning other individuals.

Users should therefore review attachments before submitting them and should avoid including unnecessary personal information.

Where an attachment contains another person's personal information, users should have an appropriate lawful basis or authorization for providing that information, where required by law.

---

## 3.6 Verification Information

Saup Angeles verifies new accounts using the following process:

* Text recognition on your identification document and facial-feature detection on your selfie are performed **on your device**. Facial detection only checks that exactly one face is present, facing the camera, with eyes open. Saup Angeles does not create or store a face template, and does not use these checks to identify you;
* Your identification photograph and selfie are then uploaded for review by an authorized administrator, who approves or rejects the account and may record a reason;
* Saup Angeles checks whether the identification number has already been used by another account, using the hash described above; and
* If your verification is rejected, you may be allowed to correct your information and submit again.

This process supports the application's verification and does not, by itself, constitute government identity verification, biometric identification, or a determination of your legal identity.

The retention of verification images is described under "Data Retention."

---

## 3.7 Guardian Information (Users Under 18)

If your date of birth shows that you are under 18, Saup Angeles also collects your guardian's name, relationship to you, and email address. See "Users Under 18 and Guardian Consent."

---

## 3.8 Responder Information

For responders, Saup Angeles processes account information, assigned station, availability status, language preference, notification token, the cases assigned to the responder, notes and photographs the responder adds, live location during active cases, and audit records of the responder's actions.

---

## 3.9 Technical, Session, and Audit Information

Saup Angeles and its supporting services process the following technical information:

* Authentication records held by Firebase Authentication;
* A notification token for your device, and your language preference, so that notifications can be delivered in your language;
* A session record (a random session identifier, your device platform, and a timestamp). Saup Angeles allows one active device per account, so signing in on a new device signs you out of the previous one;
* **Audit records** of certain actions (see "Audit Logs and Activity Records"); and
* Local data stored on your device, including your notification history (up to the 50 most recent entries), your session identifier, and app settings.

Saup Angeles does not currently include advertising or third-party analytics tools, and does not collect information for advertising or unrelated commercial profiling.

# 5. Legal Basis for Processing

Personal information must be processed on an applicable lawful basis under the Data Privacy Act.

Depending on the specific processing activity, the lawful basis may include **consent or another lawful criterion recognized under the DPA**, such as processing necessary for a contract, compliance with a legal obligation, protection of vital interests, or other applicable lawful bases.

In Saup Angeles:

* Optional sharing of your medical information with responders is based on **your separate, opt-in consent**, which you can turn off at any time;
* For users under 18, storage of personal data depends on the **consent of a parent or guardian**, given for specific purposes;
* Handling a report you submit, including sharing it with participating emergency institutions, is necessary to provide the emergency-reporting service you requested and may also protect vital interests; and
* Registration requires you to confirm that you have read the Terms and Conditions and this Privacy Policy. This confirmation is an acknowledgment and is not, by itself, consent to every processing activity.

The NPC's guidance distinguishes a privacy notice from consent: providing a privacy notice does not by itself replace the requirement for obtaining valid consent where consent is the lawful basis for processing.

Where Saup Angeles relies on consent:

* Consent should be freely given;
* Consent should be specific and informed;
* The purpose of the processing should be communicated clearly;
* Consent should be obtained through an appropriate mechanism; and
* Users should be informed of the relevant processing before it takes place.

# 10. Medical Information Sharing with Responders

Your blood type, height, weight, and vaccination details are optional. They are visible to you, and are not shown to responders unless all of the following are true:

* You have turned on the option to allow the medical responder assigned to your emergency report to view your blood type, height, and weight;
* The report is for yourself;
* The viewer is the **medical responder assigned to your report**, and the case is still open (accepted, dispatched, or on scene);
* If you are under 18, your guardian has approved medical information sharing; and
* The responder chooses to view it. Each view is recorded in the audit log.

Only blood type, height, and weight can be viewed. Vaccination information is not shown to responders through this feature.

You can turn this option off at any time. Access ends when the case is resolved.

# 11. Users Under 18 and Guardian Consent

Users under 18 may register, but their personal data is stored only with the consent of a parent or guardian.

* During account setup, a user under 18 provides a guardian's name, relationship, and email address;
* Saup Angeles emails the guardian a link, valid for 7 days and usable once. Sending a new request cancels earlier links. A user may request up to five emails in total, with a short wait between requests;
* The guardian chooses which purposes to approve: (1) storing account and profile information, (2) storing the user's location when a report is sent, (3) keeping report history, and (4) storing optional medical information so responders can use it in an emergency;
* The guardian types their full name as a signature. Saup Angeles keeps a record of the consent, including the guardian's name, the purposes approved, the version of the privacy notice shown, and the date;
* While consent is pending, or if it is later withdrawn, some features that store personal data are limited, and the user is notified; and
* A guardian who has concerns, or who wants to withdraw consent or ask about a child's data, can contact the Data Protection Officer listed in this Privacy Policy.

The consent page is hosted on Firebase and links to this Privacy Policy.

At this time, Saup Angeles does not set a minimum age for registration. Before wider deployment, the Personal Information Controller should decide whether to set one.

# 12. Responders and Live Location Sharing

Responders are authorized personnel of participating institutions. Their accounts are created and managed by authorized administrators.

* While a responder's assigned case is in the **dispatched** or **on scene** status, the Application shares the responder's precise location, updated as they move, so that dispatch and the reporter can see where help is. The Application shows a notification on the responder's device while this is happening;
* Live location is stored in the Realtime Database and is removed when the case is resolved or the responder is reassigned;
* Responders can add notes and photographs to a case, which are kept with the case record and may be seen by the people authorized to view that case; and
* Responder actions, including starting and stopping location sharing, are recorded in the audit log.

Responders must use the personal information they see only for the emergency they are assigned to.

---

# 13. Emergency Notifications and Report Status

Saup Angeles sends push notifications concerning submitted reports, account verification, and guardian consent, and sends case-assignment notifications to responders.

Report notifications show the service involved (for example, "Medical") and the new status, such as accepted, dispatched, on scene, resolved, or reopened. They do not include the details of your report.

Notifications are sent to a token that identifies your device. The token is removed when you deactivate your account or sign out.

Users should understand that notifications may be visible on a device depending on the device's notification settings.

Users are responsible for configuring their device's notification and lock-screen settings according to their privacy preferences.

Notification delivery may be delayed or unsuccessful because of:

* Internet connectivity;
* Mobile-network conditions;
* Device settings;
* Operating-system restrictions;
* Notification-service failures; or
* Other technical circumstances.

Your notification history is stored on your device only, and you can delete entries from it.

# 14. SMS Fallback

Saup Angeles may provide an SMS fallback when internet-based functionality is unavailable or unreliable.

The SMS feature prepares a message containing your Saup Angeles user ID, the incident type, your yes/no answers to the guidance questions, any landmark you entered, and the coordinates of the reported location.

The Application opens the device's native messaging application with the message addressed to the participating station's number, so that you can review and send it. Saup Angeles does not transmit the SMS from its servers.

The station number is stored on your device after your first online use so that it is available offline.

Once you send the SMS through your telecommunications provider, the information is also processed by that provider and its messaging infrastructure. A dispatcher may then log it as a report in the System. Such reports are marked as SMS reports.

The Application records in the audit log that the SMS fallback was opened, including the reference number and the destination number.

Users should review the message before sending it and should avoid including unnecessary personal information.

Applicable telecommunications charges may apply.

# 17. Audit Logs and Activity Records

To protect the System and the people who use it, Saup Angeles keeps audit records of important actions. These records are available to authorized administrators.

Examples of what is recorded include:

* Sign-in, sign-out, password changes, phone-number linking, and when a session is replaced by a sign-in on another device;
* Account creation, deactivation and reactivation, verification decisions, and changes to guardian consent;
* Report creation, status and priority changes, routing, and assignment to responders;
* When a user starts an emergency call from the app, opens the SMS fallback, or asks for directions;
* Failed report submissions (the type of error only);
* Responder notes and photographs being added, edited, or removed, and responders starting or stopping location sharing; and
* Each time a responder views a reporter's medical information.

Each record stores who did the action, what was done, which record it affected, and when. For most profile changes, the log records that a field changed and not its previous or new value. The text of responder notes and the images themselves are not copied into the log.

# 19. Personal Data Breaches

Saup Angeles will maintain procedures for responding to suspected personal data breaches.

Where a breach meets the applicable requirements for mandatory notification under the Data Privacy Act and NPC rules, the responsible Personal Information Controller will notify the National Privacy Commission and affected data subjects within the applicable period.

Under the DPA's breach-notification framework, mandatory notification generally applies when sensitive personal information or information that may enable identity fraud is reasonably believed to have been acquired by an unauthorized person and the circumstances create a real risk of serious harm. The NPC states that required notifications must generally be made within **72 hours** of knowledge of, or reasonable belief in, the qualifying breach.

Affected users will be provided with information required by applicable law, which may include:

* The nature of the breach;
* The personal information potentially involved;
* Measures taken to address the breach;
* Measures recommended to reduce potential harm;
* Contact information for obtaining additional information; and
* Other information required by the NPC.

# 27. Automated Processing

Certain Saup Angeles features use automated processing, including:

* **Priority assignment:** a report is given a priority (low, high, or critical) based on the incident type and your answers to the guidance questions;
* **Additional services:** your answers may cause an additional service to be notified. For example, an injured person in a police report, or a person trapped in a fire, can add a medical response, and a medical report in which the condition is described as intentional can add a police response;
* **Routing:** each report is sent to the nearest active station of each service involved, based on the reported location;
* **Verification checks:** text recognition on identification documents and facial-feature detection on selfies, performed on your device, and duplicate detection using the identification number hash; and
* **System-generated guidance:** pre-call questions and first-aid and safety tips.

Automated processing is intended to support the System's functions.

Automated processing should not be interpreted as a substitute for professional judgment by emergency personnel, who review reports and decide how to respond. An administrator, not the automated checks, decides whether an account is approved.

The NPC recognizes the right of data subjects to be informed about the existence of automated decision-making and profiling.
